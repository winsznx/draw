Issue once. Every LP pays its own notice.

One call, one ledger. The money moving and the notice turning funded happen in the same transaction.

  1. 01Fund manager

    Issue

    One transaction creates a private notice for each LP. Other LPs and outsiders can’t read it, even by contract id.

    A contract signed by the fund and that LP

  2. 02LP

    Pay

    The LP locks the exact amount in a Token Standard allocation that carries the notice’s reference. Nothing has moved yet.

    A CIP-0056 allocation to the fund’s party

  3. 03Fund manager

    Settle

    One transaction checks reference, payer, amount and asset, moves the coin and marks the notice funded. Anything else is refused.

    The allocation is consumed, so it can’t pay twice

  4. 04Read-back

    Verify

    A read-back step reads the settlement from the ledger and runs 14 checks. Only then does the money count as deployable.

    A login that only reads, on LocalNet and on the live site

OverdueAn LP that doesn’t pay stays unresolved. Before the due time the ledger refuses to mark it overdue. After it, the fund can. The fund can also send a notice to review, or waive an overdue or reviewed one, each with a note.

  • Privacy sits below the app

    With a login per party, as on LocalNet, LP A’s own login never gets LP B’s notice back, even by contract id. A missing filter in our code can’t change that.

  • Funded follows the money

    The notice turns funded in the same transaction that moves the coin, and that transfer needs the LP’s prior consent.

  • A payment can’t be reused

    A settled allocation is consumed. The same payment can’t fund a second notice.

  • What it doesn’t hide: the fund sees every LP by design, the node operator that hosts every party can read them all, and per the Scan docs the Canton Coin leg shows up in Scan with its random reference and the party ids, whose hints such as draw-lpa are readable, which we haven’t checked against Scan.

Go to market through the people who run the call.

Who first

One outsourced fund administrator serving several emerging managers, because one relationship reaches several funds. Then the COO or ops lead at emerging managers that run their own calls.

How we reach them

Direct outreach to fund ops people and administrators, introductions from people who run funds, and Canton ecosystem events. The interviews on slide 9 are the first pipeline.

Pricing Hypothesis

A per-fund subscription for managers, and per-call pricing for administrators who serve many small funds. We haven’t had a pricing conversation yet. Each interview asks what the fund pays for administration today.

Pilot, in three stages

  1. Stage 1

    Shadow one real call

    No money moves. DRAW runs next to the fund’s spreadsheet until the call closes. It needs commitment amounts and about an hour a day from one ops person.

  2. Stage 2

    A small test settlement

    2 or 3 LPs pay a small test amount on DevNet or TestNet from their own parties. The real call stays on the bank rail.

  3. Stage 3

    What a real pilot needs

    A settlement asset the fund and LPs accept, LPs on validators they trust, LP sign-in that gives our server no LP credentials, export to the admin system and legal review.
